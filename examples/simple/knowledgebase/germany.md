# Germany

Germany's biscuit tradition is spiced, festive, and firmly baked in December.

## Lebkuchen

Soft, rich gingerbread from Nuremberg, dating to the 14th century, where the guilds guarded the recipes fiercely; the highest grade (Elisenlebkuchen) must contain almost no flour, only nuts and spice. Flavoured with aniseed, coriander, cloves, cardamom, and cinnamon, often glazed or coated in dark chocolate, and sold on decorated hearts and figures at Christmas markets. It keeps for months, which is good, because a proper Christmas market buys enough to need it to.

## Pfeffernüsse ("pepper nuts")

Small, hard, glazed spice biscuits baked for Christmas, historically made with actual pepper along with cinnamon, cloves, and anise. They soften in the glaze rather than being dunked, which is just as well: German tradition does not dunk. Eating them in the first week of Advent is a tradition in itself, and it is never too early in Germany.

## Spekulatius

Thin, crunchy, almond-and-spice biscuits stamped with windmill or figure reliefs, related to Dutch speculaas. Eaten at Christmas, often with coffee, and also year-round in some regions, which is not approved but is tolerated.

## Butterbrot... is not a biscuit

Included only to settle arguments: it is bread. Move along.
