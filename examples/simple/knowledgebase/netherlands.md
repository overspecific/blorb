# Netherlands

The Dutch are famous for butter and spice, and their biscuits reflect both.

## Speculaas

Thin, crunchy, intensely spiced biscuits (cinnamon, nutmeg, cloves, ginger, cardamom) traditionally baked for St Nicholas' Day (6 December), stamped with scenes and figures using carved wooden moulds — windmills, ships, and Sinterklaas himself. The spice blend is sold as "speculaas spice" ready-mixed, which the Dutch consider modern and efficient. Believed to originate with Dutch East India Company spice imports, making it the most literal biscuit-of-empire in this guide.

## Stroopwafel

Two thin, crisp waffle rounds with a caramel syrup filling, originating in Gouda in the 18th century (reportedly made from bakery leftovers). The correct ritual is to rest one on top of a hot cup of coffee or tea for a minute to soften the syrup before eating; this is widely practised and almost never rushed. Sold everywhere in the Netherlands and, at this point, everywhere else.
