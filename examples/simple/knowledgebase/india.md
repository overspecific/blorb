# India

India runs on glucose biscuits and chai, at a scale no other country matches.

## Parle-G Glucose Biscuit

Possibly the best-selling biscuit on Earth — by some counts the best-selling packaged food product in the world by volume. Launched by Parle in 1939; the "G" originally stood for Glucose, now officially for Genius. Cheap, sweet, tea-shaped, and energising in the way a plain biscuit can only aspire to be: for generations of Indians, the default snack of childhood. Dunking one in chai is the national default. The child on the wrapper (the "Parle-G girl") has not aged since 1939.

## Nankhatai

Cardamom-and-ghee shortbread, crisp, sandy, and fragrant, brought to India via Dutch and Persian traders (the name is believed to derive from the Persian "nân" meaning bread). Sold in small bakeries and by street vendors, often baked fresh on hot plates; the texture varies from bakery to bakery and everyone has a favourite.

## Jeera Biscuit

A savoury cumin-seed biscuit, dry, crisp, and faintly salty-sweet, designed to be eaten with chai rather than after it. The cumin is the whole point; there is no other reason to eat one, and that is sufficient.
