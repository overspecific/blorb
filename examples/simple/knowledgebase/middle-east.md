# Middle East

The region's biscuit tradition centres on semolina, dates, nuts, and moulds.

## Ma'amoul

Semolina shortbread filled with dates, pistachios, or walnuts, pressed in carved wooden moulds (or stamped with a patterned tong) so each filling traditionally gets its own shape — round for dates, elongated for pistachios, and so on, so guests know what they're getting before they bite. Traditional across the Levant for Eid and Easter, made in huge batches at home and exchanged between households of every faith. Dusted with icing sugar. Best fresh; the semolina keeps them tender rather than crisp. The dough is often rested overnight with melted butter so the semolina fully hydrates, which is the difference between a good ma'amoul and a great one.

## Kahk

Egyptian celebration biscuits: round, crumbly, shortbread-like biscuits filled with agwa (date paste), walnuts, or plain, dusted with icing sugar. Baked in enormous quantities for Eid al-Fitr, when the whole country smells of them; the tradition is old enough that some trace it to ancient Egypt. The date-filled version keeps well; the plain ones are best in the first days.
