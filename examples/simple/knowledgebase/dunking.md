# Dunking Notes

Dunking is the practice of briefly submerging a biscuit in a hot drink (usually tea, sometimes coffee or milk) before eating it. Done right, it softens the biscuit and releases flavour. Done wrong, it ends in tragedy at the bottom of the cup.

## The physics

A dunk softens the biscuit by liquid wicking into its pores; the time to structural failure depends on the biscuit's density, fat content, and how much you care about your tea. The critical window is the time between "pleasantly softened" and "detached wreckage" — for most British biscuits it is measured in seconds, and for some it is measured in regret. The drink matters too: milk slows cooling and softens faster than plain black tea, and coffee is a harsher environment than either.

## Per-biscuit guidance

- **Rich Tea** — the best dunker in common circulation; 3–4 seconds is the standard window. Firm, forgiving, and engineered for it.
- **Digestive** — 2–3 seconds, then eat immediately; it softens fast and the window is short. Plain digestives dunk; chocolate digestives are a gamble (the chocolate acts as a partial barrier, and the debate about which way up remains unresolved).
- **Hobnob** — the oaty surface maximises tea transfer, so it softens faster than it looks like it will. 2 seconds, no lingering.
- **Ginger Nut** — longer than you think, shorter than you fear: it holds on gamely and then snaps without warning.
- **Custard Cream / Bourbon** — the filling warms and softens first; plan for it, and twist before dunking if you're that kind of person.
- **Chocolate Chip Cookie** — an American cookie is usually soft enough that dunking is unnecessary; a crisp one takes a short dip in milk by tradition rather than tea.
- **Biscotti** — they exist to be dunked; refusing is rude. Vin Santo is canonical; coffee or tea is accepted.
- **Savoiardi / Ladyfingers** — designed from the ground up for absorption (see tiramisu); tea is not the traditional medium but they take it well.
- **Scones** — not a biscuit, and cream-first vs jam-first is a separate, more violent argument.
- **Shortbread** — do not dunk. Some things are sacred. The butter content means it sheds crumbs rather than softening cleanly; respect it and eat it dry.
- **Madeleines / Langues de Chat** — fresh, delicate, and not improved by a swim. Dunking one in tea is legal in France but not advisable.
- **Tim Tam** — the Tim Tam Slam is a special case: bite opposite corners, use as a straw, and eat the whole thing before it collapses. It is not dunking so much as controlled demolition.
- **Stroopwafel** — rest on top of a hot drink for a minute to soften the caramel; this is the sanctioned technique and not technically a dunk.
- **Anything sandwiched with cream or chocolate** — the filling is the first casualty; dunk briefly or not at all.
- **Anything iced or glazed** — the icing dissolves first, on your fingers. You were warned.

## Etiquette

- The dunk belongs in your own cup. Other people's cups are not involved.
- Recovery of a sunken biscuit is permitted with a spoon; recovery by hand is discouraged in company.
- The "one second longer" instinct is responsible for the overwhelming majority of dunking failures. Trust the guidance above, not your optimism.
