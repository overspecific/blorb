# Spain

Spain's great biscuit tradition is Andalusian and Christmas-driven.

## Polvorones and Mantecados

Crumbly shortbread-style Christmas biscuits from Estepa and other Andalusian towns, made with lard (manteca — hence both names) and toasted flour, and flavoured with cinnamon, lemon, or anise. Polvorón literally means "dusty", which is exactly how they eat: one bite and it disintegrates, so tradition holds you should compress it in your fist first to hold it together. Sold year-round in supermarkets and by nuns at convent doors; the convent ones are the ones people cross town for.

## Carquinyolis

Catalan almond biscuits, twice-baked like biscotti but smaller and made with whole toasted almonds. The name comes from "carquinyoli", meaning brittle or crooked, which describes their texture precisely. Eaten at breakfast with coffee or as an afternoon merienda snack.
