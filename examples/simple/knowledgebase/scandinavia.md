# Scandinavia

Scandinavia bakes around coffee: fika in Sweden (coffee and something sweet, twice daily, as a protected institution) and its equivalents across the region.

## Danish Butter Cookies (Vaniljekranse)

The tin biscuit: crisp, buttery cookies in the famous Royal Dansk blue tin (the vanilla wreath is the signature shape), sold around the world since the 1960s. The tin is famous for being reused for sewing supplies, buttons, and important documents, to the point that the biscuit's real contents are a worldwide surprise. Danish butter quality carries the recipe: few ingredients, high standards.

## Kanelsnurr ("cinnamon whirls")

Norwegian cinnamon whirl biscuits, part of the same tradition as Swedish kanelbullar: a yeast dough rolled with butter, cinnamon, and sugar, coiled, and baked. Part of fika, the Swedish institution of coffee and something sweet, twice daily, and its Norwegian equivalent. Cardamom is a common partner to the cinnamon and is a defining flavour of Scandinavian baking in general.

## Pepparkakor

Swedish thin, crisp ginger snaps spiced with ginger, cinnamon, cloves, and often cardamom. Traditional at Christmas, where they are rolled and cut into hearts, stars, and goats, and eaten with glögg (mulled wine). Also pressed into the classic supermarket form and eaten year-round with cheese (especially the Norwegian habit of blue cheese on a pepparkakor, which is a genuine tradition and a genuinely good one).
