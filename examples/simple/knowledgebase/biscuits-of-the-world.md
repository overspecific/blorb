# Biscuits of the World

A field guide to the great biscuits (and their close cousins). Bread-based
taxonomy varies by region: much of this is contested, and that is part of
the fun.

## United Kingdom

- **Digestive** — A semi-sweet, lightly wheaty biscuit invented in 1839 in
  Scotland.
  Traditionally eaten with tea; the base of the chocolate digestive, the
  nation's most dunked biscuit. Moral debates about chocolate-up or
  chocolate-down remain unresolved.

- **Custard Cream** — A sandwich biscuit with a vanilla "custard" buttercream
  filling between two ornately embossed bars. Won "Britain's favourite
  biscuit" in a 2007 reader poll and has never let the country forget it.

- **Jammie Dodger** — Two shortbread rounds with a heart-shaped jam window.
  The jam is raspberry. It has always been raspberry.

- **Ginger Nut** — The hardest biscuit in common circulation. Excellent
  dunked for the brave, catastrophic for the overconfident: it will snap,
  not bend.

- **Hobnob** — An oaty, chewier cousin of the digestive, available
  milk-chocolate-covered or plain. The surface texture is optimised for
  maximum tea transfer.

## Italy

- **Biscotti (cantucci)** — Twice-baked almond biscuits from Prato, designed
  to be hard enough to survive storage and dunking in Vin Santo. "Biscotto"
  means "twice-cooked", which is also where the English word biscuit comes
  from.

- **Amaretti** — Small, crisp almond-and-amaretto macarons, chewier in the
  middle. Naturally gluten-free.

- **Pan di Stelle** — Star-studded cocoa-and-hazelnut breakfast biscuits,
  beloved by Italian children and nostalgic adults alike.

## France

- **Madeleine** — A small shell-shaped sponge cake-biscuit hybrid from
  Commercy; the trigger of Proust's most famous involuntary memory. Best
  eaten fresh from the oven.

- **Languille de Chat ("cat's tongue")** — Thin, buttery, elongated biscuits,
  often paired with chocolate or sorbet.

- **Palet Breton** — A dense, crumbly, salted-butter shortbread from
  Brittany. Thick, round, and unreasonably rich.

## Germany

- **Lebkuchen** — Soft gingerbread from Nuremberg, dating to the 14th
  century. Spiced with aniseed, coriander, cloves, and cardamom, often
  glazed or coated in dark chocolate. A Christmas institution.

- **Butterbrot... is not a biscuit** — Included only to settle arguments:
  it is bread. Move along.

## Spain

- **Mantecados and Polvorones** — Crumbly shortbread-style Christmas
  biscuits made with lard (manteca) and toasted flour. Polvorón literally
  means "dusty", which is exactly how they eat: one bite and it
  disintegrates.

## Scandinavia

- **Danish Butter Cookie (Vaniliekranse)** — The tin biscuit. Sold in the
  famous blue tin that generations of households around the world then
  reuse for sewing supplies.

- **Kanelsnurrede** — Swedish cinnamon whirl biscuits; part of the broader
  fika tradition of coffee and something sweet, twice daily.

## North America

- **Chocolate Chip Cookie** — Invented at the Toll House Inn, Massachusetts,
  in 1938. Whether it is a "biscuit" in the British sense is a permanent
  transatlantic border dispute. Soft or crisp is a personal moral test.

- **ANZAC Biscuit** — Technically Australasian: a hardy oat-and-golden-syrup
  biscuit sent by families to soldiers in WWI because it survived the
  voyage. Coconut inclusion is standard; raisin inclusion is a crime.

- **Snickerdoodle** — A cinnamon-sugar-dusted cookie with a tang from cream
  of tartar. The name means nothing; the biscuit does not care.

## Australia and New Zealand

- **Tim Tam** — Two chocolate-malt biscuit layers with chocolate cream,
  entirely enrobed in chocolate. The "Tim Tam Slam" (biting opposite corners
  and drinking tea through it) is the correct way to eat one.

- **Lamington** — A sponge square in chocolate and desiccated coconut. At
  the boundary of the biscuit kingdom; included for completeness.

## Middle East

- **Ma'amoul** — Semolina shortbread filled with dates, pistachios, or
  walnuts, pressed in carved wooden moulds. Traditional across the Levant
  for Eid and Easter.

## India

- **Parle-G Glucose Biscuit** — Possibly the best-selling biscuit on Earth.
  Dunking one in chai is the national default. The child on the wrapper has
  not aged since 1939.

- **Nankhatai** — Cardamom-and-ghee shortbread brought to India via Dutch
  traders; crisp, sandy, and fragrant.

## Dunking notes

- Digestives and hobnobs: 2–3 seconds, then eat immediately.
- Ginger nuts: longer than you think, shorter than you fear.
- Biscotti: they exist to be dunked; refusing is rude.
- Anything sandwiched: the filling warms and softens first. Plan for it.
- Shortbread: do not dunk. Some things are sacred.
